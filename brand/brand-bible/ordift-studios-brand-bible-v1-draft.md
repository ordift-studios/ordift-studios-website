and behaves — and, from this version on, how it thinks and operates

Ordift Studios, or could it be any creative company with the name swapped

### Brand Bible version (detailed)

Ordift Studios did not begin with a camera. It began with an eye.

Long before any commercial campaign or formal client assignment, Myredlive
Anim-Tetey had a habit of noticing moments and details that could easily
pass unnoticed — end-of-academic-year celebrations, family and social
gatherings, architecture, sunrises, sunsets, abstract art. That instinct to
observe, arrange and preserve what felt meaningful ran quietly for years
before it became a practice.

While studying at the University of Ghana, that instinct found a practical
direction. Myredlive supported the commercial, marketing and
organisational side of events and programmes within his residential hall,
while also scouting individuals for television and radio commercials.
These experiences reached further than finished creative work — they
introduced him to the planning, coordination, promotion and human
potential behind every successful production.

Around the middle of 2016, an opportunity turned that growing interest
into a creative practice. Myredlive became the sole accredited
photographer for a Hall Week celebration. The assignment produced more
than an event gallery — it produced trust. Other halls began reaching out
for photography coverage and promotional flyer design for their own
celebrations. What had begun as a natural desire to preserve moments was
becoming a professional direction.

The name Ordift was formed from the phrase "God's Gift" — letters drawn
from "gOD's gIFT" and refined, with the addition of the letter R, into a
name that could be spoken and remembered naturally. The name reflects a
conviction that creativity is a gift entrusted by God, one to be
developed, disciplined and used with purpose.

A major turning point came through a pharmaceutical product-launch
campaign. Seeing the finished work on billboards and in commercial
advertising revealed a larger possibility: creative work could do more
than document an event — it could introduce products, shape public
perception, and help a brand communicate at scale.

The vision naturally extended beyond photography. Years spent identifying
potential in people for commercials had already shown Myredlive an
instinct for recognising ability — including in people who didn't yet
recognise it in themselves. That strength, paired with a growing interest
in design, branding, film, content and artistic expression, made it clear
that photography was not the complete destination. It was the foundation
of a broader creative house.

The early period demanded patience. Building a client base often meant
taking on unpaid work to gain experience and build a portfolio, and
depending on others for visibility sometimes meant trust that wasn't
honoured. Those experiences became lessons rather than the public
identity of the company — they shaped a belief that credibility cannot be
borrowed permanently from other people. It has to be earned, through
consistent work, resilience and integrity.

Ordift Studios continues to grow from that foundation — built
deliberately as a multidisciplinary creative house where photography,
film, design, branding, content and talent operate as one connected
system. Its story is not one of instant success or overnight recognition,
but of observing carefully, starting with what was available, and staying
consistent enough for small opportunities to become a lasting vision.

> Real success takes patience, resilience and consistency.

## 7. Brand Principles

Where Values (section 6) are what Ordift Studios *believes*, these are the
operating rules that belief translates into — the things that don't bend
under deadline or revenue pressure:

1. **No unapproved claim goes public.** No invented fact, statistic,
   client, testimonial, or history — the rule that governs this entire
   document also governs everything produced from it.
2. **One creative point of accountability per project.** A single named
   person (currently the Founder) signs off before anything is delivered
   or published.
3. **Every department meets the same Creative Standards bar (section 21)
   before it's allowed to publish under the Ordift name** — a new
   department launching doesn't get a lower bar to hit while it's new.
4. **Client and talent communications get equal seriousness.** No
   double standard based on who's paying whom (Customer Experience
   Principles, section 19).
5. **Growth follows readiness, not opportunity.** A revenue opportunity
   that would require breaking principle 3 or 4 gets declined or
   restructured, not taken as-is.

### Brand Bible version (detailed)

Before Ordift Studios, before any creative title, Myredlive Anim-Tetey was
simply a young person trying to understand himself — what he was good at,
where he belonged. There was no early plan to become a creative
professional. What developed instead was a habit: paying attention. To
details, to meaningful moments, to how people responded to images and
experience, long before he had language for why any of it mattered.

His formal education ran in a different direction. In Senior High School
he studied Business — not a creative programme, but one that gave him an
early grounding in organisation, marketing, and how an idea becomes
something commercially sustainable. That grounding didn't produce his
creative eye; personal interest and lived experience did. But it shaped
how he'd come to think about creativity as something that has to be
managed and positioned, not just made — a business instinct running
alongside an artistic one from early on.

His creative influences were Ghanaian, specific, and personal. Among
photographers and creatives who've gone on to be widely recognised, one
figure stood out early: the late Bob Pixel. What drew Myredlive wasn't
just the work — it was what the work built: a name that came to stand for
creativity and excellence, an identity a Ghanaian creative built that
reached far beyond photography itself. That example — a career, not just a
skill — shaped what Myredlive believed was possible.

Faith runs under all of it. The belief that creativity is a gift — the
same belief the studio's name is built on — isn't a private sentiment. It
functions as an assignment: something not to take for granted, something
that comes with the responsibility to keep learning and stay humble
(talent may be given, but excellence still has to be earned through
discipline and consistent work), and — just as importantly — to notice the
same potential in other people. Myredlive describes this as a
responsibility that extends beyond his own work: to identify, encourage,
and help develop ability in people who may not yet see it in themselves.

That belief shows up directly in how he leads. Ordift Studios today is
founder-led, and Myredlive describes his style as hands-on, collaborative
and closely involved — in ideas, client communication, and the final
quality of every piece of work. He leads by example rather than
instruction, and makes a point of recognising what each collaborator
brings rather than treating contributors as interchangeable. At the same
time, he's deliberate about not wanting the studio to stay dependent on
him for every decision indefinitely — the stated goal is building the
standards, values and processes that let other people eventually lead
with confidence, without losing what makes the work identifiably Ordift's.

Ask him how he defines success personally, separate from any company
metric, and the answer isn't a result — it's a decision: the decision to
keep moving, or try again, after something doesn't work. Disappointment,
rejection, delay, mistakes — in his framing, choosing not to stop in the
face of any of those is already a form of success, independent of
whatever outcome eventually follows.

What he wants to be known for follows the same thread as his influences:
not simply strong creative output, but positive impact — work that helps
people see possibility in themselves, their businesses, their ideas. He
wants his name to eventually represent the same thing he admired in the
creatives who shaped him: creativity used with purpose, resilience,
integrity, and a genuine effect on the people it touches — not just the
work it produces.

**Founder Philosophy:**
> Creativity may begin as a gift, but impact is built through discipline,
> resilience and the willingness to keep trying.
>
> People often carry potential before they have the confidence or
> opportunity to recognise it. Good leadership and meaningful creativity
> should therefore do more than produce attractive work — they should help
> people see possibilities, develop their abilities, and become better
> because they were part of the process.

## 9. Company Philosophy

> A brand's visuals, film, design and voice are usually produced by
> different people who never talk to each other — a photographer here, a
> designer there, an agency for the campaign. The result looks disjointed
> because it *is* disjointed. Ordift Studios exists on the premise that
> those disciplines should be handled as one connected system, by people who
> understand how a photograph, a film, a logo and a caption are all doing
> the same job: making something memorable. That's the actual product —
> memorability, not deliverables.

**Creative Belief:**
> Equipment can expand what an artist is able to execute, but it cannot
> replace vision. Better tools remove friction and make the learning
> process easier — but creativity, discipline, patience and consistency
> are what give the work meaning.

This isn't a critique of professionals who invest in advanced equipment —
better tools are a genuine advantage. It's a statement of what the
advantage is *for*: equipment removes limitations, it doesn't supply
vision. Referenced in Photography (section 23) and Creative Standards
(section 21), since it's the underlying reason both sections judge work on
craft and intent rather than on gear.

## 11. Brand Archetype

Using the standard Jungian brand-archetype framework as a working tool
(not a factual claim about the company, a strategic lens):

**Primary: The Creator.** Driven to build something of lasting value,
values originality and craft over convenience, uncomfortable with
"good enough." This is the dominant archetype because it matches section
9's actual company philosophy — building connected systems, not
one-off deliverables.

**Secondary: The Magician.** The transformation archetype — taking a
brand's raw story and turning it into something memorable (directly
mirrors the Brand Story's "shape stories" language, section 1).

**What this should change in practice:** Creator-led brands earn trust by
showing *process and craft*, not just finished output — behind-the-scenes
content, the "how" behind a shoot or a design system, isn't a nice-to-have
content category, it's core to how this archetype builds credibility.
Magician-led messaging can lean into before/after or
raw-material-to-finished-story framing in case studies, once real ones
exist.

## 13. Competitive Landscape

## 14. Ideal Client Profiles

## 17. Writing Guidelines

- **Sentence length:** short by default; a long sentence is earned, not a
  habit.
- **Verbs over adjectives.**
- **No stacked superlatives.**
- **Numbers and specifics over vague claims**, once real numbers exist —
  never invented to fill a gap.
- **Contractions are fine in body copy, not in headlines.**
- **Active voice, always**, except where passive is the more honest framing.
- **Never invent a specific fact to fill a gap.**

## 18. Brand Vocabulary

**Preferred:**
| Say this | Not this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Creative house" | "Agency" | Undersells the in-house production/talent model |
| "One connected system" | "Full-service" | Generic agency-speak vs. the actual differentiator |
| "Department" | "Team," "division" | Matches the site IA and mother-brand structure |
| "Shape stories" / "make memorable" | "Content creation" | Commodity term every competitor uses |
| "Founder-led" | "CEO" (for now) | Matches the approved Team framing |

**Avoided:** "World-class," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class" (unearned
superlatives); "synergy," "leverage," "solutions" (corporate filler);
"vibe," "content creator" as a catch-all (too casual/generic); "affordable,"
"cheap," "budget-friendly" (undercuts premium positioning); any specific
number, date, client name, testimonial, or competitor claim not confirmed
by you.

**Naming conventions:** "Ordift Studios" on first reference; "Ordift"
acceptable after. Department names capitalized as proper nouns when
referring to the actual department.

**A guardrail on this table:** it targets specific weak, generic, or
corporate-jargon terms — it is not a license to replace plain, clear
words by default. "Client," "project," "photography," "production," and
similar ordinary nouns are correct and should be used naturally. Premium
vocabulary means intentional word choice at the moments that matter, not
avoiding everyday language everywhere.

## 21. Creative Standards

*Underlying belief: the Creative Belief in section 9 — gear removes
friction, it doesn't supply vision. These standards judge craft and
intent, not equipment.*

**Technical (photography/video):** correct exposure and white balance,
intentional focus, retouching that's invisible rather than obviously
filtered, color grading consistent with the brand palette's restraint
(section 29) rather than trend-chasing presets.

**Technical (design):** legible typography hierarchy at actual viewing
size, brand colors used per section 28's rule, files delivered in usable
formats.

**Conceptual:** a clear reason the work looks the way it looks, explainable
in one sentence.

**The publish test:** would the Founder be comfortable if a prospective
client's first impression of Ordift Studios was *this specific piece*? If
the honest answer is "it's fine, not great," it's a client delivery, not a
portfolio piece.

## 22. Service Philosophy

- **Scope is explicit, not implied.**
- **Bespoke by default, packaged where it helps the client.**
- **Revision policy** is a business and legal decision that must be

## 23. Department Philosophies

**Photography** — A photograph is a claim about how something should be
remembered. The job isn't "take good pictures," it's deciding what's worth
remembering before the shutter ever clicks. This is vision-led work, not
equipment-led — see the Creative Belief in section 9.

**Videography** — Film is photography with time added, and time is what
most brand video wastes. Every second earns its place or gets cut.

**Graphic Design** — Design that isn't used consistently isn't design,
it's a one-time favor. The job is building systems a client can run with.

**Branding & Creative Strategy** — Most "branding" work is decoration
applied after the real decisions were made elsewhere. This department
exists so the decisions and the decoration come from the same place.

**Content Creation** — Content produced in a single monthly scramble looks

## 35. Decision-Making Philosophy

The meta-principles behind how decisions get made at Ordift Studios,
independent of any single decision:

- **Decisions are made at the most immediate reasonable level.** Nearly
  everything currently runs through the Founder — matching the hands-on

## 36. Internal Decision Framework

A working filter for whether to take on a project, publish a piece of
work, or approve a creative direction:

1. **Capability check:** does this fit what at least one department can
   actually deliver today, per the Creative Standards (section 21)?
2. **Portfolio check:** would we want this in the public portfolio, or is
   it good client work that isn't a showcase piece?
3. **Consistency check:** does saying yes here conflict with something
   already promised to another client?
4. **Brand-fit check:** does this project let Ordift Studios say yes
   without diluting the positioning in section 2?
